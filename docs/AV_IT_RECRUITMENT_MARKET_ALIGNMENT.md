## Market findings

### AV

Recruiters and AVIXA consistently separate the AV lifecycle into design, project delivery, installation, commissioning/programming, service/support and live events. “AV Engineer” is frequently used as an ambiguous vacancy title, so matching must use the responsibilities beneath the title.

Recurring role families are:

1. AV systems design, consultancy and pre-sales: requirements, surveys, signal flow, drawings, BOM, estimation and design handover.
2. Project engineering: buildability review, technical submittals, coordination, RFIs, changes and as-built information.
3. Project management: scope, programme, commercial control, resource, risk, client communication and acceptance. This does not imply programming or commissioning competence.
4. Installation and rack build: fabrication, cabling, mounting, drawings, labelling, testing and closeout.
5. Commissioning and field engineering: power-up, configuration, test, fault isolation and acceptance.
6. Control and DSP programming: manufacturer-specific tools, code/configuration, protocols, backups and handover.
7. Field service: break-fix, preventive maintenance, configuration recovery, SLA and service evidence.
8. Onsite AV/VC support: meeting-room readiness, live user support, first-line recovery and ticketing.
9. Live events: preparation, rigging, show operation, presentation/video/audio workflows and de-rig.

Technology names are commonly decisive qualifiers: Crestron, Extron, AMX, Q-SYS, Biamp, Dante, Teams Rooms, Zoom Rooms, AutoCAD and D-Tools recur as requirements. They are knowledge prerequisites, not job roles.

### IT

The UK Government framework and recruiters group IT hiring into architecture, data, IT operations, product/delivery, quality engineering and software development, with cyber security as a distinct profession. Current recruiter demand particularly highlights cloud, DevOps/platform, cyber, data and AI alongside established support and infrastructure roles.

Recurring role families are:

1. Service desk and end-user computing: first-line triage is distinct from second-line endpoint engineering and device management.
2. Network, systems and infrastructure: network devices, server/virtualisation, core services, backup and operational reliability require separate evidence.
3. Cloud engineering: provider-specific resources, networking, IAM, infrastructure as code, observability and cost.
4. DevOps/platform engineering: CI/CD, containers, orchestration, infrastructure automation and developer enablement.
5. Site reliability engineering: SLOs, automation, incident response, capacity and resilience.
6. Cyber security operations: SIEM/EDR triage, investigation, response and vulnerability workflows. This must not be inferred from general IT support.
7. Software engineering: stack, testing, APIs/data and delivery practices.
8. Data engineering and analytics: pipelines/platform engineering are distinct from analysis, semantic modelling and BI.
9. QA/test engineering: test analysis, automation, API/integration validation and defect evidence.
10. Technical architecture: end-to-end solution decisions and assurance, distinct from hands-on implementation.
11. IT project/delivery management: delivery governance and transition, distinct from IT operations management.

## Rules adopted in TechSubbies

- Account type, sector profile, job role, responsibility level, skill and product knowledge are separate fields.
- A job carries a canonical role id plus searchable market aliases.
- Responsibilities explain what the client actually expects and are searchable.
- Skills are rated as practical competence and cannot grant a role implicitly.
- Products/software/manufacturers carry a minimum practical-experience level and may be hard prerequisites.
- Management, architecture and project delivery roles do not imply hands-on specialist engineering.
- General AV/IT profiles are limited to general support opportunities.
- Evidence is associated with the claimed role capability and technology experience.
- Every specialist role has a separate knowledge layer describing concepts and product ecosystems the engineer must understand, not merely tasks they have encountered.
- Every role publishes explicit boundaries and adjacent-role distinctions. For example, installation is not commissioning; project management is not project engineering; service desk is not endpoint engineering; cloud engineering is not DevOps; and DevOps is not SRE.
- Knowledge topics identify whether a client may make a named software, platform, protocol or manufacturer requirement a hard prerequisite.

## Roles added from this review

AV: Systems Designer/Pre-Sales Engineer, Project Engineer, Project Manager, Field Service Engineer, Onsite AV/VC Support Technician and Live Events AV Technician.

IT: End User Computing/Desktop Engineer, Cloud Engineer, DevOps/Platform Engineer, Site Reliability Engineer, Cyber Security/SOC Analyst, Data Engineer, Data Analyst/BI Developer, QA/Test Engineer, IT Project/Delivery Manager and Technical/Solution Architect.

## Future extensions

Add roles only when supported by real demand in the platform’s target market. Likely extensions include residential smart-home integration, broadcast/media systems, lighting control, database administration, ERP/CRM consulting, penetration testing, governance/risk/compliance, machine-learning engineering, product management and business analysis. These should remain distinct role profiles rather than keyword expansions of adjacent roles.
